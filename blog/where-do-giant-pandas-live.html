Where do giant pandas live in the wild?

Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026 · Last reviewed 20 November 2026 · By the PandasWise team

Wild giant pandas live only in China, in cool, wet bamboo forest on six mountain ranges in Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu. They are gone from the lower ground they once used, and the IUCN lists them as extinct in two further Chinese provinces, Hubei and Hunan. Sources on this page were checked on 20 November 2026.

Six ranges, three genetic groups

WWF UK describes the wild range as remote, high mountain bamboo forest in six ranges across those three provinces. The ESABII Data Portal, run by Japan's Biodiversity Center, names the ranges: Minshan, Qinling, Qionglai, Liangshan, Daxiangling and Xiaoxiangling. It says about 75 percent of the population lives in Sichuan. The Smithsonian's National Zoo describes the same country as a few mountain ranges in south central China.

The IUCN Bear Specialist Group sorts those six ranges into three genetic populations, not six separate species. The Qinling mountains are one. The Minshan mountains are the second. The third is a single, highly fragmented population spread across Qionglai, Daxiangling, Xiaoxiangling and Liangshan. The group says most researchers do not treat the Qinling animals as their own subspecies. The family question, bear or not, is settled separately and is covered in are giant pandas bears?

The province line is sharper than the mountain line. ESABII's 75 percent in Sichuan is a population share, not a claim that the other two provinces are empty. Qinling is the Shaanxi stronghold. Gansu holds the northern edge of the Minshan country. The giant panda guide puts these places next to size, diet and status.

How high, in feet and in metres

Two official ranges are in print, and they are not copies of each other. The National Zoo says pandas live in broadleaf and coniferous forest with a dense bamboo understory, at 5,000 to 10,000 feet, in country marked by heavy rain or dense mist and often wrapped in cloud. Five thousand feet is about 1,520 metres. Ten thousand feet is about 3,050 metres.

The ESABII portal gives 1,200 to 4,100 metres above sea level, and says 1,500 to 3,000 metres is more typical. The IUCN Red List assessment of 11 April 2016, still the current assessment when this page was checked, prints the same limits: 4,100 metres at the top and 1,200 metres at the bottom. The Smithsonian band sits inside that wider span, and it sits close to the band ESABII calls typical. It does not raise the ceiling to 4,100 metres. Where a page on this site needs one number, it should say which source it is using. Bamboo type and density decide which slope inside that belt a panda actually uses. The habitat guide goes further into forest type and the national park.

Where they no longer live

The National Zoo says pandas once lived in lowland areas, and that farming, forest clearing and other development now restrict them to the mountains. The 2016 IUCN assessment is more specific about provinces. It lists the species as extant, meaning still living there, in Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu, and as extinct in Hubei and Hunan. Extinct here means gone from those provinces, not extinct as a species. The species label is Vulnerable, which the conservation guide and the endangered status page explain with the latest counts.

ESABII describes the six ranges as isolated from one another, so pandas in different ranges do not form one freely mixing herd. A 23 November 2025 Xinhua report, quoting Liu Guohong of China's National Forestry and Grassland Administration, said the Giant Panda National Park had brought 73 reserves into one park of about 22,000 square kilometres and protects about 1,340 wild pandas. Xinhua's 14 June 2026 report of the administration's 2025 data put the national wild total near 1,900. Those are different counts. The park is the main protected block inside the three provinces. It is not a roster of every wild panda. Whether the remaining forest is shrinking is a separate question: is giant panda habitat shrinking?

Moving up and down the mountain

Giant pandas do not hibernate. ESABII says they generally move to lower elevations in winter, and that they may shelter in hollow trees, rock crevices and caves. Do pandas hibernate? covers the winter behaviour. The seasonal shift stays inside the same mountain belt. It is not a migration out of China.

Zoos are not the wild range

Wild pandas live only in China. Pandas in zoos in other countries are breeding and research animals under cooperation agreements. They are not extra wild populations, and a zoo address is not part of the six ranges. The checked list of those zoos is where to see pandas. Each row there is tied to an official zoo page and a date, because loans change.

Compare with red panda range

Wild giant pandas live only in China. Red pandas live in mountain forest in Nepal, India, Bhutan, Myanmar and China, which is a wider map and a different animal. See where red pandas live and the side by side comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Where do giant pandas live in the wild?

In six mountain ranges in Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu provinces in China. The IUCN 2016 assessment, still current when checked on 20 November 2026, lists the species as extinct in Hubei and Hunan.

How high up do giant pandas live?

The Smithsonian's National Zoo gives 5,000 to 10,000 feet, about 1,520 to 3,050 metres. The ESABII portal and the IUCN give 1,200 to 4,100 metres, and ESABII says 1,500 to 3,000 metres is more typical.

Which province has the most wild pandas?

Sichuan. The ESABII portal says about 75 percent of the population lives there.

Do wild pandas live outside China?

No. Wild giant pandas are found only in China. Pandas in zoos elsewhere are not wild populations.

Why don't pandas live in lowlands any more?

The National Zoo says farming, forest clearing and other development restrict them to the mountains.
