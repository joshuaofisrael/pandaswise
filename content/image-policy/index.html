This page explains how PandasWise uses pictures. Every photo and photo style image on the site is one of two kinds, described below, and the small Source link under an image leads back to this page or to the matching row on our photo credits page.

Real licensed photographs

Most of the pictures on PandasWise are real photographs of giant pandas and red pandas. Every one of these comes from Wikimedia Commons under a free licence: public domain, CC0, or CC BY SA with attribution. Each photo shows a real panda, not a staged or recreated scene, and each one is listed on our photo credits page with its file name, photographer and licence. We checked the licence on the photo's own Commons page before using it. These photos are not AI generated, and the caption under each one names its source, such as "CC0 photo by Mathias Appel" or "Public domain photo".

AI generated images

Some of the pictures on PandasWise are AI generated images: illustrations made with an AI image generator so they look like realistic wildlife photographs of giant pandas or red pandas. They are not photographs of a real individual panda, they do not show a real event, and they are not pictures of real people. Every caption under one of these images says in its own text that it is an AI generated image and not a real photograph, so you never have to guess from a link alone. The AI generated images section of our photo credits page lists each one, what it shows and which pages use it.

How to tell which is which

The caption always says. A real photo's caption names a photographer and a licence, such as Mathias Appel or J. Patrick Fischer, and links through to the full record on the photo credits page. An AI generated image's caption states plainly that it is an AI generated image and not a real photograph. If a caption does not say AI generated, the picture is a real, licensed photograph of a real panda.
