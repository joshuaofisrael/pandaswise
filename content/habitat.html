Wild giant pandas live only in China, in cool, wet mountain forests with a dense bamboo understory in Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu, mostly between about 1,200 and 3,500 m. Red pandas live farther west and south, in temperate Himalayan and southwest Chinese forests across Nepal, India, Bhutan, northern Myanmar and China, generally above about 1,200 m.

Giant panda range

Giant pandas once lived in lowland areas too, but farming, forest clearing and other development have pushed them into the mountains. Today they survive in six mountain ranges: the Minshan, Qionglai, Liangshan, Daxiangling and Xiaoxiangling mountains in Sichuan and neighbouring Gansu, and the southern slopes of the Qinling in Shaanxi. Most wild pandas live in Sichuan.

What giant panda habitat looks like

  • Elevation: mostly 1,200 to 3,500 m (4,000 to 11,500 ft) according to San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance; the Smithsonian gives 5,000 to 10,000 ft (about 1,500 to 3,000 m).
  • Climate: damp and misty, with heavy rain or fog for much of the year.
  • Forest: broadleaf and coniferous forest with a dense bamboo understory. San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance research found good panda habitat needs old growth conifer forest, at least two bamboo species and access to water. Old trees provide hollow logs and stumps big enough for dens.
  • Neighbours: WWF describes the region's biodiversity as unparalleled in the temperate world. Protecting panda forest also protects golden snub nosed monkeys, takin, multicoloured pheasants and many other species, which is why the giant panda is called an umbrella species.

The Giant Panda National Park

China established the Giant Panda National Park in 2021. It spans parts of Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu and joined 73 previously separate nature reserves into about 22,000 square kilometres of protected land. According to China's National Forestry and Grassland Administration, it protects about 1,340 wild giant pandas, links the habitats of 13 local populations, and includes ecological corridors so pandas can move and breed between groups. About 72 percent of wild giant pandas are now under strict protection, and total habitat area has grown to about 2.58 million hectares.

Part of this landscape is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site: the Sichuan Giant Panda Sanctuaries (Wolong, Mount Siguniang and the Jiajin Mountains), inscribed in 2006.

Why fragmentation matters

Roads, railways, dams and farmland have split panda forest into islands. Small, isolated groups are more exposed to inbreeding, disease, local bamboo die offs and chance events. That is why corridors that reconnect populations are a core part of current conservation. More on the conservation page.

Red panda range

Red pandas live in high, temperate forests with bamboo understories from Nepal through the Indian states of Sikkim, West Bengal and Arunachal Pradesh, Bhutan and northern Myanmar to Tibet, Sichuan and Yunnan in China. WWF says almost half of red panda habitat is in the Eastern Himalayas, and about 38 percent of the total potential habitat is in Nepal. Where scientists recognise two species, the Himalayan red panda lives mainly in Nepal, India and Bhutan, and the Chinese red panda mainly in southwest China.

Red panda habitat needs water nearby, suitable tree cover at the right altitude, and enough bamboo. These conditions are increasingly hard to find as forest is cleared or grazed, and bamboo grows poorly in degraded forest. Langtang National Park in Nepal and Singalila National Park in India are among the protected areas with red pandas.
