As of 8 October 2026, giant pandas can be seen in zoos in 16 countries outside mainland China (15 hosting pandas on loan from China, plus Mexico) and in Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan. The United States has the most of any country, six pandas at three zoos. Inside China, the best known places are the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding and the panda bases at Wolong and Bifengxia. Red pandas are far more widespread in zoos worldwide.

Panda loans start, end and change often, and individual animals can be off show for health checks or quarantine. We checked every entry below against an official zoo or government announcement or a major news report, and we update this page when things change, but always check the zoo's own website before you travel.

Recent changes worth knowing

  • September 2026: Zoo Atlanta received Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, two years after its previous pandas went home. They go on show on 6 November 2026.
  • 2026: Everland's Shubao was born in June; Taman Safari's cub Rio made his debut in June; Zoo Negara's new pair went on show in January.
  • January 2026: Japan's last giant pandas left Ueno Zoo, leaving Japan without pandas for the first time since 1972.
  • November and December 2025: Beauval's adult pair returned to China early after Huan Huan was diagnosed with a minor kidney problem; Pairi Daiza's Hao Hao went to China for medical care.
  • Coming up: Macao's twins Jian Jian and Kang Kang (November 2026) and Everland's twins Rui Bao and Hui Bao (December 2026) are moving to China. Singapore's pair and Indonesia's current agreement reach their scheduled dates in 2027.

Countries with no giant pandas right now

Some countries that were once famous for pandas have none at the moment, including the United Kingdom (Edinburgh Zoo's pair returned in December 2023), Japan (since January 2026), Canada (Calgary Zoo's pandas returned in 2020) and Finland (Ähtäri Zoo's pair returned in November 2024). If you are in one of these countries, look for red pandas instead.

Seeing giant pandas in China

Mainland China holds by far the most giant pandas in human care, 808 at the end of 2025 according to official figures, spread across breeding centres and many city zoos. The best known places for visitors are in Sichuan:

  • Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, in the northern suburbs of Chengdu. Many pandas born abroad, and pairs returning from overseas loans, go to Chengdu. Book through the official site; reservations and visitor limits apply at busy times such as Chinese public holidays.
  • China Conservation and Research Center for the Giant Panda (CCRCGP) bases, including Wolong Shenshuping in the Wolong reserve and Bifengxia near Ya'an. These are in mountain settings closer to wild panda habitat and further from the city.
  • Wild habitat. Wolong, Mount Siguniang and the Jiajin Mountains form the UNESCO listed Sichuan Giant Panda Sanctuaries. Seeing a wild giant panda is very rare even for researchers, but the Giant Panda National Park landscape is spectacular in its own right.

Where to see red pandas

Red pandas are kept by many more zoos than giant pandas, across North America, Europe, Asia and Australia, usually as part of coordinated breeding programmes for this Endangered species. Many cities without giant pandas, including in the UK and Japan, have red pandas; check your nearest accredited zoo's animal list. Two notable options in red panda range countries:

  • Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park, Darjeeling, India, which runs a red panda conservation breeding programme that supports reintroduction into nearby forests such as Singalila National Park.
  • Red Panda Network ecotrips in eastern Nepal, small group trips with local guides who track wild red pandas. The organisation is clear that sightings are possible but never guaranteed.

Tips for seeing pandas

  • Plan around activity. Giant pandas alternate long feeding bouts with naps of a few hours, so check the zoo's feeding or keeper talk times. Red pandas are most active at dawn and dusk.
  • Check the zoo's news page the week you go. Pandas can be moved off show for quarantine, breeding season or health checks, as Macao's twins are from 9 October 2026.
  • Expect timed entry for new arrivals. New panda exhibits often use timed tickets or queues in the first months.
  • Look up first. Red pandas and young giant pandas often rest high in trees.
