Giant pandas and red pandas, explained

PandaWise is a free, plain language guide to the world's two pandas. The giant panda is a black and white bear from the mountains of central China that lives almost entirely on bamboo and is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. The red panda is a much smaller, tree climbing mammal from the Himalayas and southwest China; it is not a bear, it sits in its own family, and it is listed as Endangered. Quick facts

  • China's National Forestry and Grassland Administration put the wild giant panda population at nearly 1,900 in 2025, up from about 1,100 in the 1980s (conservation).
  • About 99% of a giant panda's diet is bamboo, and it spends 10 to 16 hours a day foraging and eating (diet).
  • A newborn giant panda weighs roughly 85 to 140 g, about 1/900th of its mother's weight (cubs).
  • The red panda was described by Western science in 1825, decades before the giant panda, and is the only living member of the family Ailuridae (red panda).
  • As of 8 October 2026, giant pandas live in zoos in 16 countries outside mainland China plus Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan (where to see them).
